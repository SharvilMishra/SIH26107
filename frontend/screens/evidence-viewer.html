What are the specific grounding requirements for residential electrical installations according to IS 732?

smart_toy
verified Verified Response

According to IS 732 (Code of Practice for Electrical Wiring Installations), the grounding requirements for residential installations stipulate that all exposed conductive parts of the electrical equipment must be connected to the protective earthing terminal.

Specifically, the resistance of the earth continuity conductor from the main earthing terminal to any point in the installation should not exceed 1 ohm.

IS 732 : 2019 BUREAU OF INDIAN STANDARDS

5.4 Earthing Arrangements

5.4.1 Every installation shall be provided with an earthing terminal to which all the protective conductors of the installation shall be connected.

arrow_right_alt

5.4.2 Earth Continuity Conductor Resistance

The resistance of the earth continuity conductor from the main earthing terminal to any point in the installation, including the connection to the exposed conductive part, shall not exceed 1 ohm. This ensures rapid operation of protective devices in the event of a fault.

5.5 Protective Devices

5.5.1 The characteristics of the protective devices for automatic disconnection of supply shall be selected to satisfy the conditions of the protective measure used.

5.5.2 For TT systems, residual current devices (RCDs) shall be used for fault protection. Overcurrent protective devices may be used for fault protection provided that a suitably low value of fault loop impedance is permanently and reliably assured.

5.6 Equipotential Bonding

5.6.1 Main equipotential bonding conductors shall connect the main earthing terminal to the following extraneous-conductive-parts:
a) metallic water service pipes;
b) metallic gas installation pipes;
c) other metallic service pipes and ducting;
d) metallic central heating and air-conditioning systems;
e) exposed metallic structural parts of the building.
